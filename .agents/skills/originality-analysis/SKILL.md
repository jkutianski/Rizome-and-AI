# Originality Analysis

## Purpose

Analyze the originality of a work at the level of its conceptual operation, methodological construction, cartographic perspective, argument, and relations among ideas.

The purpose is not to determine whether the author has invented every concept used in the work.

Instead, ask:

> **What does this work do with its concepts, relations, and perspective that is genuinely distinctive?**

Originality may arise from:

* a new question;
* a distinctive perspective;
* a new relation among established concepts;
* a methodological operation;
* a transformation of an existing concept;
* a particular cartographic architecture;
* an unusual displacement of perspective;
* a reflexive consequence;
* a new way of making an existing problem visible.

---

# Core Principle

Originality does not require conceptual invention from nothing.

A work can be original even when its individual concepts are established elsewhere.

Conversely, a work can use unusual terminology or combine many references without performing an original conceptual operation.

Therefore:

> **Originality belongs primarily to what the work does with its materials, not merely to the materials it contains.**

---

# Scope

Analyze originality at multiple levels:

* question;
* perspective;
* conceptual operation;
* methodological operation;
* cartographic construction;
* relational architecture;
* treatment of AI;
* treatment of technology;
* treatment of the rhizome;
* observer implication;
* reflexivity;
* argument;
* synthesis;
* voice where relevant.

Do not turn this into a literature review unless external comparison is explicitly required.

---

# What This Skill Does Not Do

Do not:

* demand absolute novelty;
* assume originality requires new terminology;
* reward obscurity;
* reward complexity;
* reward the number of concepts;
* reward the number of citations;
* equate interdisciplinary combination with originality;
* assume unusual structure is original;
* claim priority over existing scholarship without evidence;
* invent precedents or comparisons;
* evaluate originality solely through external similarity detection.

Originality analysis is primarily a reconstruction of the work's own operation.

---

# Originality vs Novelty

## Step 1 — Distinguish Novelty from Originality

A formulation may be new without being conceptually significant.

A familiar concept may become original through a new operation.

Distinguish:

* new wording;
* new combination;
* new application;
* new perspective;
* new relation;
* new transformation;
* new methodological use;
* new explanatory structure.

The strongest forms of originality generally concern what the work makes possible to see or think.

---

# The Central Question

## Step 2 — Reconstruct the Work's Distinctive Operation

Ask:

> **What is the work actually doing?**

Possible operations include:

* reframing;
* relocating;
* connecting;
* differentiating;
* reversing perspective;
* destabilizing a category;
* mapping;
* tracing relations;
* exposing tensions;
* transforming a concept;
* making an implicit relation visible;
* introducing reflexivity;
* changing analytical scale.

Do not select an operation merely because it sounds theoretically sophisticated.

Ground it in the actual text.

---

# Originality of the Question

## Step 3 — Analyze the Initial Question

Determine whether the central question is:

* generic;
* established;
* reformulated;
* distinctive;
* unusually positioned.

For this project, distinguish between:

> “Is AI a rhizome?”

and the more specific operation:

> **What happens if the rhizome is used as a way of observing how AI emerges, deploys, transforms, and expands through multiple relations?**

The distinction matters because the latter changes the status of the rhizome from object to perspective.

Do not assume the example is original merely because it is stated differently. Verify what the question actually does in the work.

---

# Perspective as Originality

## Step 4 — Analyze the Observer's Position

A work may become distinctive by changing who or what is being observed and from where.

Analyze whether the work:

* treats the rhizome as object;
* treats it as analytical perspective;
* makes the observer part of the cartographic operation;
* recognizes the map as constructed;
* preserves the excess of the territory over the map.

The combination of these operations may constitute originality if the text actually develops them.

Coordinate with `map-territory` and `observer-implication`.

---

# Conceptual Operation

## Step 5 — Identify Transformations of Established Concepts

For each central concept, ask:

1. What does the concept ordinarily do?
2. What does it do in this work?
3. Has its function changed?
4. Has it been placed in a new relation?
5. Does that relocation produce analytical consequences?

Relevant concepts may include:

* rhizome;
* map;
* territory;
* assemblage;
* node;
* inter-stem node;
* substrate;
* emergence;
* propagation;
* agency;
* knowledge;
* authority;
* relevance.

Do not claim that the work “reinvents” a concept when it merely applies an established one.

---

# Rhizome as Perspective

## Step 6 — Test the Project's Central Operation

Specifically test whether the work's originality lies in using the rhizome as a **way of seeing** rather than as a property attributed to AI.

Ask whether the work actually maintains:

* AI ≠ rhizome;
* rhizome as analytical perspective;
* map as provisional construction;
* territory as exceeding the map;
* relational dynamics;
* tensions;
* transformations;
* asymmetry;
* contingency.

If these distinctions organize the work, determine whether their combination produces a distinctive analytical operation.

Do not call the operation original solely because it invokes Deleuze and Guattari.

---

# Cartographic Originality

## Step 7 — Analyze the Map Architecture

Examine whether the architecture itself performs an original operation.

Potentially distinctive elements include:

* domains as cartographic orientation rather than ontological categories;
* assemblages as relatively stable configurations;
* nodes as concentrations rather than topics;
* inter-stem nodes as transformations crossing assemblages;
* propagation as transformation extending through relations;
* map stability coexisting with territorial dynamism.

Ask:

> Does this architecture merely classify material, or does it change how the material can be understood?

This distinction is central.

---

# Node as Conceptual Operation

## Step 8 — Analyze the Node Concept

Determine whether “node” functions merely as:

* a topic;
* a chapter;
* a category;
* an important element.

Or whether it functions as:

> **a concentration of relations and tensions.**

If the latter is consistently developed, assess whether this relational criterion produces a distinctive way of organizing the material.

Do not infer originality from the label “node.”

---

# Inter-Stem Originality

## Step 9 — Analyze the Inter-Stem Concept

Determine whether inter-stem nodes are merely interdisciplinary topics such as:

> AI + Biology

or whether they represent:

* relations across assemblages;
* tensions;
* concentration;
* modification of conditions;
* transformation;
* possible propagation.

The originality question is not whether “AI + biology” is a new topic.

It is whether the work provides a distinctive **relational mechanism for identifying cross-assemblage transformations**.

Coordinate with `inter-stem-validation`.

---

# Reflexive Originality

## Step 10 — Analyze the AI-Writing Loop

If AI participated in producing the work, analyze whether this participation becomes conceptually consequential.

Potential operation:

> AI is part of the object of analysis while also participating in constructing the map through which that object is observed.

Ask:

* Does the text merely disclose AI assistance?
* Or does the disclosure change the analysis?
* Does it alter the observer position?
* Does it create a reflexive loop?
* Does it raise a substantive question about cognition, mediation, authorship, or the map?

A disclosure is not automatically an original contribution.

Its analytical consequence is what matters.

Coordinate with `ai-writing-reflexivity`.

---

# Originality of Distinctions

## Step 11 — Identify Productive Distinctions

A work may be original through distinctions that prevent conceptual collapse.

Relevant distinctions include:

* map / territory;
* rhizome / map;
* element / relation;
* relation / tension;
* concentration / node;
* node / theme;
* inter-stem / interdisciplinarity;
* connection / propagation;
* propagation / causality;
* causality / determinism;
* complexity / emergence;
* causal participation / intention;
* operational autonomy / mental autonomy;
* production / knowledge;
* production / evidence;
* relevance / authority;
* authority / legitimacy;
* authority / truth;
* technical possibility / historical inevitability.

Do not call every distinction original.

Ask whether the distinction performs real analytical work.

---

# Originality Through Restraint

## Step 12 — Analyze What the Work Refuses to Claim

Originality can sometimes lie in avoiding conventional shortcuts.

For example:

* refusing to call AI a rhizome;
* refusing to treat every important element as a node;
* refusing to equate interdisciplinarity with inter-stem structure;
* refusing to treat propagation as simple diffusion;
* refusing to equate emergence with novelty;
* refusing to treat AI participation as authorship by default;
* refusing technological determinism;
* refusing to treat the map as neutral.

Ask whether these refusals produce positive analytical consequences.

A list of exclusions alone is not originality.

---

# Originality and Architecture

## Step 13 — Test Whether the Whole Is More Than the Sum of Its Concepts

Ask whether the work's originality emerges from the relation among:

* question;
* perspective;
* map;
* assemblages;
* nodes;
* inter-stem transformations;
* propagation;
* observer implication;
* reflexivity.

A work may contain no individually novel concept and still perform a distinctive synthesis.

But “synthesis” should not automatically be treated as originality.

The synthesis must generate an analytical operation that would not be visible in the same way if the components were merely listed.

---

# Originality and Connections

## Step 14 — Analyze Relational Originality

Look for distinctive relations among established concepts.

Examples:

* map stability ↔ territorial dynamism;
* observer ↔ map construction;
* AI ↔ extended cognition;
* AI-writing ↔ observer implication;
* biological representation ↔ intervention;
* technical capacity ↔ geopolitical capacity;
* mediation ↔ relevance ↔ authority;
* production ↔ evidence ↔ attribution.

Ask:

> Does the connection merely place two concepts next to each other, or does it produce a new analytical consequence?

---

# Originality and Existing Traditions

## Step 15 — Situate Without Overclaiming

When external knowledge is available or explicitly requested, determine whether the operation:

* reproduces a well-established approach;
* resembles an established framework;
* extends an existing discussion;
* combines established approaches;
* appears distinctive;
* potentially conflicts with an established precedent.

Do not claim:

* “no one has ever done this”;
* “this is completely original”;
* “this is the first work to…”

unless adequately established by external evidence.

Originality can be described proportionally:

* distinctive;
* potentially distinctive;
* unusual;
* established;
* derivative;
* unclear.

---

# Comparative Originality

## Step 16 — Use External Comparison Carefully

External comparison is appropriate when determining whether an operation is genuinely distinctive.

Compare:

* the question;
* analytical perspective;
* conceptual definitions;
* methodological architecture;
* treatment of AI;
* treatment of rhizomatic thought;
* cartographic construction.

Do not compare merely keywords.

Two works can use identical vocabulary while performing very different operations.

Conversely, two works can use different vocabulary while performing substantially the same operation.

---

# Evidence Threshold

## Step 17 — Match Originality Claims to Evidence

Distinguish:

### Textual originality

What is distinctive within the work itself.

### Comparative originality

What appears distinctive relative to identifiable external traditions or works.

The first can often be assessed from the text.

The second requires external evidence.

Never infer comparative priority from absence of examples in the analyst's knowledge.

---

# Originality vs Influence

## Step 18 — Distinguish Influence from Derivation

A work can be strongly influenced by:

* Deleuze and Guattari;
* STS;
* philosophy of technology;
* philosophy of mind;
* systems theory;
* extended cognition.

Influence does not eliminate originality.

The relevant question is what the author does with those influences.

---

# Originality vs Interdisciplinarity

## Step 19 — Do Not Equate Combination with Originality

Combining:

* philosophy;
* biology;
* AI;
* geopolitics;
* sociology;

does not automatically constitute originality.

Ask whether the combination changes the analytical relations among the fields.

Coordinate with `structural-architecture` and `inter-stem-validation`.

---

# Originality vs Complexity

## Step 20 — Reject Complexity as Evidence of Originality

A more elaborate architecture is not automatically more original.

A larger number of:

* concepts;
* nodes;
* sections;
* relations;
* distinctions;

does not prove originality.

Ask whether additional complexity produces additional analytical capacity.

---

# Originality vs Unconventionality

## Step 21 — Distinguish Unusual from Original

A formulation may be:

* unconventional but conceptually weak;
* unconventional and productive;
* conventional but exceptionally well executed;
* familiar but newly configured;
* obscure without being original.

Unusualness is evidence of difference, not evidence of intellectual originality.

---

# Originality and Voice

## Step 22 — Analyze Authorial Distinctiveness

Voice can contribute to originality when:

* personal experience changes the conceptual trajectory;
* the author's situatedness affects the map;
* the style carries a distinctive mode of inquiry.

Do not confuse literary individuality with conceptual originality.

Coordinate with `voice-style-rhythm`.

---

# Originality and Negative Space

## Step 23 — Analyze What Is Absent

Sometimes the work's distinctive operation becomes visible through what it does not do.

Possible absences:

* no claim that AI is a rhizome;
* no linear causal chain among A–E;
* no universal conclusion;
* no independent “feedback” node;
* no autonomous “desires” node;
* no conventional interdisciplinary taxonomy.

Treat absence cautiously.

Absence becomes analytically significant only when the surrounding architecture makes the omission meaningful.

Coordinate with `evidence-and-citation`.

---

# Originality Vulnerabilities

## Step 24 — Identify Claims That May Overstate Originality

Watch for formulations implying:

* unprecedentedness;
* complete conceptual novelty;
* unique methodology;
* first occurrence;
* radical rupture with existing thought.

Ask whether the text establishes such claims.

If not, classify the problem as a scope or evidential issue rather than simply rejecting the underlying operation.

Coordinate with `falsifiability-vulnerability`.

---

# Counterfactual Originality Tests

Apply controlled tests.

## Remove the Rhizomatic Perspective

What remains?

If the architecture becomes an ordinary classification of AI topics, the rhizomatic perspective may be doing substantial work.

## Replace “Node” with “Topic”

What analytical capacity disappears?

## Remove the Inter-Stem Criterion

Does the cross-assemblage architecture become ordinary interdisciplinarity?

## Remove Observer Implication

Does the map become a conventional external analysis?

## Remove Reflexive AI-Writing Analysis

Does the work lose a meaningful consequence concerning cognition or mediation?

## Replace the Architecture with Conventional Chapters

Which conceptual operations disappear?

These tests help determine whether originality resides in the architecture rather than terminology.

---

# Common Failure Modes

## Novelty Inflation

Treating any unusual formulation as original.

## Citation Counting

Assuming many references demonstrate originality.

## Conceptual Name Inflation

Assuming a new label creates a new concept.

## Interdisciplinary Inflation

Treating combination of fields as originality.

## Complexity Inflation

Treating a larger architecture as a deeper contribution.

## Comparative Overclaim

Claiming unprecedentedness without evidence.

## Influence Denial

Treating influence by established traditions as incompatible with originality.

## Originality by Obscurity

Treating difficulty as evidence of conceptual novelty.

## Synthesis Inflation

Calling every synthesis original.

---

# False Positives

Do not classify as original merely because:

* terminology is unfamiliar;
* structure is unconventional;
* the work combines many fields;
* concepts appear in an unusual order;
* prose is highly personal;
* the author uses many distinctions;
* AI assistance produced unusual formulations.

---

# False Negatives

Do not overlook originality merely because:

* concepts are established;
* the work cites established thinkers;
* the architecture is relatively simple;
* the vocabulary is familiar;
* the work does not claim novelty explicitly.

Originality may reside in the relation among established components.

# Output Protocol

For each significant finding, report:

### Originality Dimension

Identify the level:

* question;
* perspective;
* conceptual operation;
* distinction;
* architecture;
* relational configuration;
* methodology;
* reflexivity;
* argument;
* synthesis;
* voice.

### Evidence

Identify the textual basis.

### Existing Function

Explain what the relevant concept or operation does in established form when that comparison is supported.

### Work-Specific Operation

Explain what the work does differently or distinctively.

### Analytical Consequence

Explain what becomes possible, visible, or different because of that operation.

### Comparative Status

When external comparison is available, classify as:

* established;
* strongly derivative;
* familiar but reconfigured;
* distinctive;
* potentially distinctive;
* unclear.

Do not claim priority without evidence.

### Classification

Use:

* **A — Critical:** the work makes a major originality claim that is materially unsupported or the alleged distinctive operation collapses under examination.
* **B — Important:** the claimed distinctive operation is substantially weaker or more derivative than the text suggests.
* **C — Local:** a limited formulation or originality claim needs qualification.
* **D — Distinctive but Limited:** a real distinctive feature exists but should not be inflated into a general originality claim.
* **E — Non-problematic:** the operation is genuinely distinctive, appropriately scoped, or the text makes no problematic originality claim.

### Confidence

Use:

* High
* Medium
* Low

### Implication

Explain what the finding means for the work's intellectual distinctiveness.

### Recommendation

Choose:

* Necessary
* Advisable
* Optional
* Not recommended
* Leave unchanged

---

# Intervention Threshold

Do not force the author to add originality claims.

Do not insert references merely to prove that the work is original.

Do not redesign the architecture simply to make it look more innovative.

Intervene only when:

* the work overclaims novelty;
* a supposedly distinctive operation is actually inconsistent;
* the originality depends on a distinction that the text does not sustain;
* external comparison materially changes the assessment.

If the work contains a distinctive operation without naming it as “original,” preserve that restraint.

---

# Final Principle

> **Originality is not the invention of isolated concepts. It is the distinctive work performed by the relations among concepts, perspectives, questions, and methods.**

A familiar concept can become analytically distinctive through relocation.

A new term can remain conceptually empty.

A complex architecture can be conventional.

A simple architecture can produce a new way of seeing.

Therefore:

> **Do not ask only whether the work says something new. Ask whether it makes something visible, thinkable, or relationally intelligible in a way that its previous configuration did not.**

And finally:

> **The strongest claim of originality is often not “this has never been thought before,” but “this work changes what becomes visible when these relations are observed together.”**
