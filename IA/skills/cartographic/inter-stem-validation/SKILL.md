# Inter-Stem Validation

## Purpose

Determine whether a proposed **inter-stem node** is genuinely a concentration of relations and tensions that crosses assemblages or domains and produces a transformation in their conditions of functioning.

An inter-stem node is **not** simply:

* an interdisciplinary topic;
* a relation between two domains;
* an important theme;
* a section titled "AI + X";
* a two-way influence;
* a broad social consequence;
* a connection between technologies and society.

Its defining property is **transformative relational crossing**.

The central question is:

> **Does a concentration of relations and tensions cross assemblage boundaries in a way that modifies the conditions under which the assemblages function, producing a transformation that can propagate?**

---

# 1. Governing Definition

An inter-stem node is:

> **a concentration of relations and tensions that traverses two or more assemblages or domains and modifies the conditions of functioning of the assemblages involved, producing a meaningful transformation and potentially generating further propagation.**

The defining sequence is:

**assemblages → relations → tensions → concentration → modification of conditions → transformation → possible propagation**

This is an analytical sequence, not necessarily a chronological or strictly causal sequence.

---

# 2. Relation to Ordinary Nodes

A node and an inter-stem node are not the same category.

### Ordinary Node

A concentration of relations and tensions within an assemblage or primarily within one cartographic territory.

### Inter-Stem Node

A concentration whose dynamics cross assemblage or domain boundaries and **change the conditions under which those assemblages function**.

Therefore:

**every inter-stem node is a relational concentration, but not every relational concentration is inter-stem.**

Do not upgrade an ordinary node merely because it has external connections.

---

# 3. Fundamental Rule

The title cannot establish inter-stem status.

The following are insufficient:

* "AI + Biology";
* "AI + Philosophy";
* "AI + Politics";
* "AI + Education";
* "AI + Society";
* "AI + Knowledge";
* "AI + Geopolitics."

The title identifies a possible territory of investigation.

The analysis must reconstruct the dynamic.

---

# 4. Strict Validation Sequence

Validate candidates in this exact order:

### 1. Assemblages

Which assemblages are involved?

### 2. Relations

What relations connect them?

### 3. Tensions

What tensions emerge from those relations?

### 4. Concentration

Why do those relations and tensions form a recognizable concentration?

### 5. Modification of Conditions

How does the concentration modify the conditions under which one or more assemblages function?

### 6. Transformation

What changes as a consequence of that modification?

### 7. Possible Propagation

Does the transformation generate further changes elsewhere?

Failure at an earlier level weakens the case for inter-stem status.

---

# 5. Assemblage Identification

Identify the actual assemblages involved.

An assemblage is a relatively stable configuration of:

* elements;
* relations;
* tensions;
* practices;
* infrastructures;
* institutions;
* norms;
* capacities;
* material conditions.

Do not treat a broad domain such as "society" as an assemblage without reconstructing the configuration involved.

Possible assemblages may include:

* scientific research;
* biological design;
* computational infrastructure;
* educational evaluation;
* knowledge production;
* institutional regulation;
* geopolitical strategy;
* epistemic authority.

The exact assemblages must come from the text.

---

# 6. Boundary Crossing

Determine whether the candidate genuinely crosses assemblage boundaries.

A boundary crossing exists when relations move between distinct configurations whose conditions of functioning are not identical.

Examples may involve:

* scientific practices ↔ technological systems;
* biological research ↔ computational design;
* computational capacity ↔ geopolitical strategy;
* information mediation ↔ institutional authority;
* AI-assisted production ↔ knowledge evaluation.

The fact that two topics are mentioned together does not establish boundary crossing.

---

# 7. Relation Across Assemblages

Identify the concrete relations crossing the boundary.

Possible relations include:

* dependency;
* transfer;
* adaptation;
* mediation;
* substitution;
* coordination;
* competition;
* restriction;
* feedback;
* resource dependence;
* institutional incorporation;
* technical modification;
* epistemic reorganization.

Avoid vague formulations such as:

> "AI connects these fields."

Specify:

> **How does the relation operate?**

---

# 8. Tension Across Assemblages

Identify the tension generated by the crossing.

Possible tensions include:

* capacity vs constraint;
* innovation vs regulation;
* openness vs sovereignty;
* speed vs verification;
* automation vs attribution;
* representation vs intervention;
* technical capability vs institutional control;
* production vs evidence;
* relevance vs authority;
* strategic capacity vs material dependency.

The tension must belong to the relation between configurations, not merely be a general theme.

---

# 9. Concentration

Determine why the cross-assemblage relations form a concentration rather than a loose collection.

Ask:

* Which relations converge?
* Which tensions intensify?
* What common dynamic connects them?
* What makes the crossing structurally significant?
* Does the same transformation appear through several relations?
* Is there a recognizable relational center without assuming a hierarchical center?

Concentration does not require symmetry or complete connectivity.

---

# 10. Modification of Conditions

This is the decisive inter-stem test.

Identify the **conditions of functioning** that change.

Conditions may include:

* technical capacities;
* available resources;
* institutional procedures;
* evaluation criteria;
* infrastructure;
* standards;
* dependencies;
* decision environments;
* access conditions;
* material constraints;
* epistemic criteria;
* strategic possibilities;
* organizational practices.

Ask:

> **What could this assemblage do, how could it function, or under what conditions could it operate before the transformation?**

Then:

> **What condition has changed?**

Do not accept vague claims such as:

> "AI transforms biology."

Specify what condition of biological research, design, experimentation, interpretation, or intervention has changed.

---

# 11. Transformation

Identify the transformation produced by the changed conditions.

Possible transformations include:

* representation → intervention;
* technical capacity → strategic capacity;
* information mediation → altered relevance and authority;
* production → evidence → attribution;
* changed evaluation practices;
* altered institutional dependency;
* new material requirements;
* changed research workflows;
* redistributed decision capacity.

The transformation must be connected to the preceding relations and modified conditions.

---

# 12. Propagation

Propagation is possible but not mandatory.

A candidate can be a strong inter-stem node even if the text does not demonstrate a complete propagation pathway.

However, if propagation is claimed, identify:

* what transforms;
* where it moves;
* through which relation;
* what condition changes elsewhere;
* what new dynamic emerges.

Use:

`propagation-analysis`

for detailed propagation validation.

---

# 13. Reciprocal Modification

Many inter-stem dynamics are reciprocal.

For example:

**X modifies AI → AI modifies conditions of X**

But reciprocity is not required to be symmetrical.

Distinguish:

* reciprocal;
* bidirectional;
* asymmetric;
* iterative;
* feedback-driven.

Do not impose equal influence on both sides.

---

# 14. The Transformation Heuristic

A useful heuristic is:

**X modifies AI → AI modifies conditions of X → a new tension appears → that tension may propagate**

This is a heuristic, not a mandatory causal chain.

The actual text may instead contain:

* feedback;
* delayed effects;
* branching;
* partial transformation;
* interrupted transformation;
* recursive modification;
* asymmetric effects.

Use the heuristic to investigate the dynamic, not to force the text into a predefined structure.

---

# 15. Connection Is Not Inter-Stem

A strong connection between assemblages does not automatically constitute an inter-stem node.

Distinguish:

**Connection**

A meaningful relation exists.

**Inter-Stem Node**

A concentration of relations and tensions crosses assemblages and transforms their conditions of functioning.

Therefore:

> connection is necessary for inter-stem analysis, but connection alone is insufficient.

---

# 16. Interdisciplinarity Is Not Inter-Stem

Interdisciplinary work may involve several fields without producing an inter-stem node.

For example:

> AI is used in biology.

This establishes interdisciplinary relevance.

It does not establish an inter-stem node.

A stronger case would show:

* computational representation enters biological practice;
* that representation changes the conditions of biological intervention;
* intervention generates new technical requirements;
* those requirements modify computational systems;
* the resulting configuration creates new tensions.

The node lies in the **transformation of relations and conditions**, not in the coexistence of disciplines.

---

# 17. Theme Is Not Inter-Stem

A broad theme such as:

> AI and geopolitics

does not establish inter-stem status.

The analysis must identify a transformation such as:

**technical capacity → strategic capacity**

and then reconstruct:

* computational capacity;
* infrastructure;
* state or institutional actors;
* strategic dependencies;
* material resources;
* geopolitical relations;
* altered conditions of strategic action.

---

# 18. Cross-Domain Does Not Automatically Mean Inter-Stem

Crossing domains is suggestive but insufficient.

The domains:

* Science;
* Technology;
* Society;

are cartographic orientation tools.

An inter-stem node requires a transformation across configurations, not simply a passage from one label to another.

---

# 19. Material Conditions

Inter-stem dynamics must be tested against material and infrastructural conditions where relevant.

Consider:

* compute;
* energy;
* water;
* semiconductors;
* data centers;
* networks;
* devices;
* storage;
* logistics;
* minerals;
* labor;
* supply chains.

Materiality may:

* enable;
* constrain;
* slow;
* accelerate;
* redistribute;
* generate dependency;
* create strategic tension.

Do not add materiality merely because it is theoretically desirable.

It must participate in the actual dynamic.

---

# 20. Power

Inter-stem dynamics may redistribute power.

Analyze:

* capacity;
* dependency;
* access;
* control;
* authority;
* infrastructure;
* standards;
* institutional position;
* resource concentration.

Do not assume the inter-stem field is horizontally distributed.

Rhizomatic connectivity can coexist with strong concentrations of power.

---

# 21. Contingency

An inter-stem transformation is not automatically inevitable.

Check:

* investment;
* policy;
* regulation;
* standards;
* institutional choices;
* accidents;
* technical limitations;
* material scarcity;
* resistance;
* adoption;
* non-adoption;
* alternative pathways.

Distinguish:

**technical possibility ≠ historical inevitability**

---

# 22. Causality

An inter-stem node may contain causal relations.

Do not eliminate causality merely because the analysis is rhizomatic.

Instead distinguish:

* cause;
* contribution;
* enabling condition;
* constraint;
* mediation;
* feedback;
* transformation;
* propagation.

Avoid turning the inter-stem sequence into:

**A causes B causes C causes D**

unless the evidence genuinely supports that structure.

Coordinate with:

`causality-propagation`.

---

# 23. Emergence

An inter-stem transformation may involve emergence, but emergence must be demonstrated separately.

Do not infer:

> cross-assemblage complexity = emergence.

Use:

`emergence-analysis`

when emergence is explicitly claimed or analytically necessary.

---

# 24. Node E — Knowledge Evaluation

When evaluating the candidate involving knowledge, preserve the distinction:

**production → evidence → attribution**

Do not collapse this into:

* production = knowledge;
* relevance = truth;
* authority = truth.

An inter-stem analysis may concern changing conditions of knowledge evaluation without claiming that AI literally produces knowledge.

---

# 25. Node D — Power, Relevance, Authority

When evaluating D, preserve:

**mediation → selection → visibility → relevance → authority → power**

Do not make "truth production" the central criterion of inter-stem validation.

The inter-stem transformation concerns changes in relevance and authority conditions and their relation to power.

---

# 26. Node B — Biology

For B, the central transformation is:

**representation → intervention**

The validation must establish how relations between AI and biological assemblages change the conditions of biological design or intervention.

Do not validate B merely because AI is useful for biological prediction.

---

# 27. Node C — Geopolitics and Materiality

For C, the central transformation is:

**technical capacity → strategic capacity**

The analysis should consider:

* infrastructure;
* compute;
* resources;
* state or institutional relations;
* dependency;
* strategic capacity.

Do not reduce geopolitics to abstract political discussion.

---

# 28. Node A — Philosophy of Mind

For A, the central transformation is:

**observable capacities → attribution of mental capacities**

The inter-stem analysis must distinguish:

* functional performance;
* observable behavior;
* cognitive attribution;
* understanding;
* consciousness;
* agency.

Do not treat successful performance as automatically establishing mentality.

---

# 29. Node Differentiation

Before validating an inter-stem node, compare it with existing nodes.

Ask:

1. What transformation is distinctive here?
2. What assemblages are specifically involved?
3. What relations are unique?
4. What tension is distinctive?
5. What conditions change?
6. Could the same analysis be absorbed elsewhere?
7. Would validating it duplicate another node?

An inter-stem node should have a unique analytical function.

---

# 30. Architectural Consequences

Determine whether the candidate:

* changes conditions within one assemblage;
* changes relations between assemblages;
* modifies another node;
* creates a new dependency;
* opens a new propagation pathway;
* reorganizes the map;
* reveals a relation previously hidden.

The candidate should have a meaningful architectural consequence.

---

# 31. No Linear A–E Sequence

The A–E nodes must not be interpreted as:

**A → B → C → D → E**

They do not form a developmental ladder or causal chain.

Important connections may include:

* A ↔ E;
* B → C;
* C ↔ D;
* D ↔ E;
* A → D, more weakly.

These are examples of potentially significant relations, not mandatory links.

Do not force a complete network.

---

# 32. Candidate Status

Classify the candidate as:

### Strong Inter-Stem Node

All major elements of the sequence are clearly supported.

### Plausible Inter-Stem Node

The cross-assemblage transformation is strong but one or more stages remain underdeveloped.

### Ambiguous Inter-Stem Candidate

Crossing exists, but transformation or condition modification is insufficiently clear.

### Cross-Assemblage Connection

A meaningful cross-assemblage relation exists, but it does not justify node status.

### Interdisciplinary Theme

Several fields are involved, but no transformative concentration is established.

### Ordinary Node

The concentration remains primarily within one assemblage.

### Insufficient

Evidence does not justify inter-stem status.

---

# 33. Counterfactual Tests

### Remove the Cross-Boundary Relation

Does the candidate still exist?

If yes, it may be an ordinary node rather than an inter-stem node.

### Remove the Condition Modification

Does the candidate still produce an inter-stem transformation?

If no, the condition modification is constitutive.

### Replace the Title

If "AI + X" is removed, does the relational dynamic remain recognizable?

If not, the candidate may be thematic rather than inter-stem.

### Absorption Test

Can the candidate be absorbed into an existing node without losing the cross-assemblage transformation?

### Propagation Test

Does the transformation create another changed condition elsewhere?

Propagation is not mandatory, but if claimed it must be demonstrated.

---

# 34. Common False Positives

Do not classify as inter-stem merely because:

* two domains are mentioned;
* two disciplines interact;
* AI is applied to another field;
* there is interdisciplinary research;
* the topic is socially important;
* the topic is broad;
* the section has a plus sign in its title;
* both directions are discussed;
* technology changes practice;
* several assemblages are adjacent;
* the topic could theoretically affect many areas.

---

# 35. Common False Negatives

Do not reject an inter-stem candidate merely because:

* the transformation is asymmetric;
* propagation is incomplete;
* the boundary is porous;
* the transformation is distributed;
* the relation is indirect;
* the effect is mediated;
* the transformation is contingent;
* the candidate lacks a simple causal chain.

Inter-stem dynamics can be:

* distributed;
* recursive;
* asymmetric;
* mediated;
* interrupted;
* contingent.

---

# 36. Evidence Requirements

Prefer evidence in this order:

1. explicit assemblage identification;
2. explicit cross-assemblage relations;
3. explicit tensions;
4. explicit condition modification;
5. explicit transformation;
6. strong inferential reconstruction;
7. weak inferential reconstruction.

The stronger the architectural claim, the stronger the evidence required.

---

# 37. Transversal Review

Before creating, preserving, splitting, merging, or eliminating an inter-stem node, perform a transversal review.

Ask:

1. Is this actually a node?
2. Does it genuinely cross assemblages?
3. Does the crossing modify conditions?
4. Is the transformation distinct?
5. Is the function already represented elsewhere?
6. Would absorption preserve the transformation?
7. Would merging erase a meaningful distinction?
8. Does the candidate create artificial symmetry?
9. Does it create hidden hierarchy?
10. Does it increase cartographic precision?

Do not create an inter-stem node merely because the architecture appears to need another one.

---

# 40. Final Validation Test

Before assigning inter-stem status, answer all seven questions:

1. **Which assemblages are involved?**
2. **What relations cross them?**
3. **What tensions arise?**
4. **What becomes concentrated?**
5. **Which conditions of functioning change?**
6. **What transformation results?**
7. **Does that transformation potentially propagate?**

If questions 1–6 cannot be answered with sufficient evidence, do not classify the candidate as a strong inter-stem node.

Question 7 is not mandatory for node status, but any claimed propagation must be independently supported.

---

# 41. Final Principle

> **An inter-stem node is not an interdisciplinary topic. It is a transformative concentration that crosses assemblage boundaries.**

> **The decisive question is not whether two territories are connected, but whether their relation changes the conditions under which they function.**

> **Do not infer inter-stem status from adjacency, importance, or interdisciplinarity. Reconstruct the transformation.**

> **The title can point toward a possible crossing. Only the relational dynamics can validate it.**
